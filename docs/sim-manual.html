I.The tick & the pipeline

Decides: nothing — this is the clockwork the actors run inside. Order is load-bearing.

The server owns the whole world. Every real tick (20 Hz), a clock turns real elapsed time × the speed dial into dtSim sim-seconds (0 when paused). The sim then advances the world in fixed 0.05 s substeps — so 10× fast-forward is literally ten 1× steps, identical dynamics at every speed world.js:182. A single ordered list of 22 systems runs once per substep systems.js:27; the order below is exact, and it matters — production happens before pricing, pricing before dispatch, every fleet moves before the shore guns and the collision solver read final positions, and the magistrate's daily policy loop runs after governance (reading fresh loyalty/civ) and before havens.

The client is a pure viewer: it merges server snapshots by id and never simulates SimClient.js:7. The only authoritative thing a viewer can change today is the clock.

III.The Port — the economy engine

Decides: what a port makes, what it charges, and whether its people eat or breed. Runs blind (no will) — pure functions of stock and population.
  • Produce. Each island mines its primary raw at 10/s and secondary at ×0.25, with a logistic taper toward a 2000 stockpile cap production.js:17. Recipe goods scale to population (0.07 × pop) but are clamped so ≥25% of any local raw survives as exportable surplus — this is what forces trade production.js:46-62. Finished industrial goods (Weapons, Clothing, Luxury, Ships) now flow from mutable workshops — their output is further scaled by each workshop's condition × staffing, so a squeezed port's manufacturing visibly rots (see §V). Food and Ale are excluded — survival goods stay always-on, never workshop-gated, so a poor port can never starve itself into a famine spiral production.js:37-56.
  • Consume. Population eats 0.011 food/head, plus comfort goods (Ale, Clothing, Weapons, Luxury) burned as demand sinks so every port keeps importing population.js:28-41. On top of comfort, four goods gain a second, demand-scaling sink so none merely piles to its cap: Clothing is issued as crew slops (worn at sea, re-issued at port like grog) crew.js; Weapons feed a standing garrison militia that drills daily — scaled by disorder — even in peacetime upkeep.js; Luxury (with some ale) is broken out at magistrate festivals (see §V) policy.js · population.js; and Meat is now a premium food raw (a higher Food yield than Grain, so importers prefer it) rather than a pure Grain substitute production.js · economy.json.
  • Live or die. Starvation deaths are rate-bounded and floored at pop 5; growth is gated on food security (2+ days stored) so population can never outrun its larder population.js:44-57. There is no true death-spiral here.
  • Price. Mid price = base × clamp((target÷stock)^1, 0.33, 3.0), eased toward target by a non-overshooting 10%/s limiter; bid/ask are ±5% pricing.js:13-26.
  • Bank. Income = 2.4 × pop × (0.4 + 0.6·civ), scaled by (1 + tax·TAX_INCOME_W); sinks are ship upkeep + a per-workshop upkeep bill (WORKSHOP_UPKEEP_PER_DAY·pop each — this replaces the old flat PROD_UPKEEP aggregate, so a farm with no industrial works now pays zero factory upkeep), and any gold above 40 × pop overflows and is destroyed. A corrupt magistrate also skims a slice of income into a hidden hoard here. Gold is deliberately not conserved upkeep.js:25-92.

Trap

Food is spoiled twice per substep. population decays Food by 0.0003·h and then upkeep decays all stock — Food included — by another 0.0004·h population.js:32 · upkeep.js:45. Food loses ~2.3× the decay rate of every other good, tightening the very margin that drives the "starving tail."

Sound

Starvation is floored and growth is food-gated, so no runaway death-spiral. Every good/gold trade move goes through the conserved transfer primitive — the only sanctioned gold source/sink is upkeep (plus development & contract escrow).

Sinks

Every trade good now has a genuine demand sink, not just background spoilage. Raws drain into recipes (plus Wood/Fiber into ship repair and Wood/Iron into hulls); Food → population, crew rations, raids, aid; Ale → comfort + crew grog + festivals; Clothing → comfort + crew slops; Weapons → comfort + combat/shore fire + garrison militia; Luxury → comfort + festivals (which finally pull its one-consumer input PreciousMetal through the chain); Ships → fleet purchases (producer-throttled at SHIP_STOCK_CAP). Meat is a demanded premium raw rather than a redundant Grain substitute. So no good sits inert at its cap.

IV.The Port — buying hulls ✓ repriced & unblocked

Decides: how a port replaces lost ships. The old ~3× mispricing is fixed, and the parallel fleet-buyer is folded into the magistrate's policy budget.

A port grows its fleet two ways: opportunistically, by sending an idle hull to a shipyard on a voyage; or deliberately, via the magistrate's naval-expansion lever — fleet-buying is no longer a split-brain development system but one ambition-scored action inside the single treasury-budgeted policy loop (development.js is retained only as the executor the lever calls). The pricing bug (#1) is fixed: TARGET_OVERRIDES.Ships = 3 matches the yard's stock cap 3, so the ratio is 3÷3 = 1 and the multiplier sits near 1× — a hull asks its named base (~1200g) instead of the old pinned ~3780g. That drops the buy threshold from pop ≈ 120 down to pop ≈ 57, and the magistrate's tax + public-works levers give a modest port an emergent path to accumulate toward a hull (#2).

V.The Magistrate — the policy loop & mutable industry

Decides: a port's economic ambition — which now drives real actions across five levers (industry, tax, garrison, infrastructure, trade), not just a demand curve — plus whether the populace stays loyal or burns the docks, and how much a corrupt ruler quietly skims.

Each island is ruled by a named magistrate with three traits (firmness, generosity, integrity) and one of six agendas — grow, industry, wealth, fortify, splendor, order — chosen by the island's nature magistrate.js:69-85. The agenda retargets the economy: it rewrites the port's targets[] (fortify boosts Weapons ×2.4, splendor Luxury ×2.2, etc.), and those targets are the port's dynamic demand that the trade planner reads. Food is always floored at base — no agenda can starve the port magistrate.js:114-125. New: the magistrate is no longer a one-decision-per-regime actor. A daily policy loop (below) turns that ambition into concrete decrees — building works, setting taxes and tariffs, garrisoning, funding public works — reasoning about the wider world only through the island's own stale beliefs/intel (no omniscience), so a governor can be as wrong as any captain.

Loyalty eases toward a steady value set by civ, skill, generosity and lawlessness, dragged down by famine, plague, crowding and graft, buoyed by "bread & circuses" (Ale+Luxury in stock) magistrate.js:258-266. Below loyalty 0.25, unrest accrues; once it passes a skill-scaled grace window, the island rebels — halting all production and income for exactly one day — then resolves to a quell or an overthrow magistrate.js:272-318.

Mutable industry — workshops, slots & condition

An island's finished-goods industry is no longer fixed at world-gen. A port's industrial goods (Weapons, Clothing, LuxuryGoods, Ships) now live in mutable workshops that can be built, switched, demolished and repaired. island.workshops = [{good, condition}] is the single source of truth; the old island.produces[] is now a derived cache rebuilt by the one mutator mutateWorkshops() that every path routes through island.js · production.js:37-56. Food and Ale are deliberately excluded — survival goods stay always-on (never gated by labour or funding), so a poor port can never decay its own food supply into a famine spiral.

  • Slots bound the works. slotCap = min(6, 2 + popTiers + development), floored by what's already built — a seed port (pop 80) starts at SLOT_BASE = 2 with vacant berths to industrialise into; passing 150 / 400 / 900 / 1800 population each grants one more, and the magistrate can buy development slots economy.json:102-105.
  • Workshops need labour + upkeep, and rot when starved. Each demands LABOR_PER_WORKSHOP = 10 from a labour pool of pop × WORKFORCE_FRAC(0.5), so staffing = min(1, pool ÷ Σdemand) — an over-built or famine-shrunk port can't crew them all. Each bills WORKSHOP_UPKEEP_PER_DAY(0.05)·pop (this replaced the flat PROD_UPKEEP aggregate); ship upkeep is paid first, the rest funds the works. A workshop's condition eases toward min(staffing, funding) at 0.15/day (like morale/loyalty), and its output scales condition × staffing. Derelict (condition ≈ 0) it makes nothing but holds its slot until repaired or demolished upkeep.js:62-92.
  • Three status bytes drive the UI + silhouette. st ∈ {running, idle, derelict} is precomputed server-side (idle = unstaffed/unfunded or input-starved; derelict = condition ≈ 0), streamed with each workshop, and drives the InfoPanel status gauge and the map building sprite (disrepair changes the silhouette, not just tint) snapshot.js · WorldRenderer.js.

The daily policy loop — five levers

Decides: once per day, the single capital action (plus a few cheap standing knobs) a magistrate takes to serve its ambition, its treasury, and its people. All cross-island reasoning runs on the island's own stale beliefs/intel — the loop honours the no-omniscience contract.

A new policy system (policy.js, daily-gated, runs after governance) skips any island that's a haven, in rebellion, or lacking a magistrate. For the rest it first turns the continuous knobs — tax, garrison, tariff, export holds — then, once off a per-island cooldown and above a treasury reserve, takes at most one capital action down a strict priority ladder. scoreGood ranks a candidate workshop as 1.0·margin + 0.9·ambition-fit + 0.2·welfare − 0.6·import-risk − 0.5·saturation + 0.3·diversity; ambition is a strong steer (weight 0.9 — the feature thesis), while the saturation term (how many known ports already make the good) keeps industry from homogenising into all-Luxury/all-Weapons policy.js:87-370 · economy.json:562-581.

IX.The Pirate — the hunt

Decides: a pirate's every move via a strict priority ladder, coloured by a fearsome captain's traits and sharpened by hunger.

Any hull can raise the black flag — a seeded raider, a haven's forge, a prize capture, a mutiny that turns rogue, a fallen haven's home fleet, or an honest captain's own turn to piracy (maybeTurnRogue, weighed each substep for a genuinely bold + greedy master) — always gated by canTurnPirate against a single unified pirateBudget (base 8% of the fleet, lifted by active havens — see §XII #5) piracy.js:99-115. X.Combat — how a fight resolves

Decides: who wins a broadside exchange, and whether the loser sinks, strikes her colours, or is boarded and taken as a prize.

Combat is attrition, not instant. Each round (every 1.2 s while in range) both ships exchange fire; damage scales with the strength ratio and is divided by hull armour. Doctrine differs by flag: pirates chain-shot the rig to cripple-and-board, the navy pounds the hull to sink, merchants shoot rig to flee piracy.js:364-382. Fighting strength blends a base, gunnery skill (the dominant term, ×1.2), morale, gun count, boldness, a flag bonus, ship class and hull condition piracy.js:78-96. Between exchanges each captain re-weighs the fight (assessFlee): break off only when hull and odds have both turned against her — later the bolder and more seasoned the captain, never if fearless, and never if dismasted (no sail to run under). The odds are now weighed across the local balance of force when the caller supplies its grids (its consorts vs the other side's pack), so a hunter winning 3-v-1 doesn't sheer off and one losing 1-v-3 breaks sooner — falling back to the old exact self-vs-foe test when called 1-v-1. It is the same running assessment a hunted pirate and a losing privateer both make, every round piracy.js:assessFlee · balanceOfForce.

Every blow also erodes structural soundness — a per-track ceiling (hullSound/rigSound) that sits above the working hull/rig (hull ≤ hullSound ≤ 1). A fraction of each hull/rig point lost is permanent until a real dry-dock (hull wears faster than rig — framing is less recoverable than canvas), floored at SOUNDNESS_FLOOR so a jury-rigged hull always stays seaworthy enough to fight or flee. At sea a jury-rig, and even a foreign yard, can only nurse a track back up to its soundness (and no higher than JURYRIG_REACH); only a ship's HOME port — or a pirate's own HAVEN — rebuilds soundness toward whole. So a ship that has been through several hard fights becomes progressively less repairable away from a dry-dock, and havens/home ports become the thing every battered hull must eventually seek repair.js:damageHull/juryRig/repairAtPort.

Self-repair & aid — one mechanism (a boat is a boat). Every hull mends the same way: at a dry-dock (a port, or a pirate's haven), or by HEAVING TO at sea. Merchant, pirate, and privateer alike, a badly-hurt ship with repair timber aboard and no dry-dock in reach lies-to DEAD IN THE WATER and jury-rigs from its own hold — the same careen state, badge, and jury-rig ceiling for all of them repair.js:maybeHeaveToRepair. A merchant normally just docks to mend, so it only heaves-to when genuinely stranded and hurt; a raider does it often (havens are sparse). There is no separate "repair while sailing" path. Aid at sea (the mercy valve — a passing ally answering a ship in distress, relationship-gated by carried alliances) is a supply drop, not an on-the-spot patch: the helper hands SPARE canvas, timber & victuals into the stricken ship's own hold, and she mends herself with them (heaving-to if stranded) — so a rescue recovers her gradually, at her own hands and within her own soundness, never a teleport back to full repair.js:renderAid · ship.js:updateShip.

XI.The Navy — danger, bounty & privateers

Decides: the economy's self-balancing answer to piracy — how feared waters get, who funds a hunter, and how that hunter behaves.
